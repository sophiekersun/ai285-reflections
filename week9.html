Week of October 19, 2026

Week 9

This week I used three AI tools together while doing research on social enterprises in developing countries for my thesis. I used ChatGPT, Claude, and NotebookLM, and each of them did something different.

I used ChatGPT and Claude to help me find relevant research papers. I then uploaded more than 30 of those papers into NotebookLM and used it to analyze themes across them, identify research gaps, and develop potential thesis questions.

I also used ChatGPT to help me write the prompts I gave NotebookLM. This was new to me. Instead of using one tool to do the work, I used one tool to help me get more out of another. The prompts it wrote were much more specific than what I would have written on my own, and several of them told NotebookLM to separate what the papers actually showed from what it was inferring.

The part that required the most from me was the synthesis. The themes and key ideas NotebookLM gave me back were very scattered. They were not wrong exactly, but they were too broad and did not hold together as a direction I could use. I had read most of the papers myself, so I knew enough about the context to push back and ask for something narrower. After a few rounds of that, the response was much more useful.

I was only able to do that because I had already read the papers. If I had uploaded 30 sources I had not read and taken the first synthesis at face value, I would not have known that it was too scattered to be useful. That has been the pattern all semester, but it was clearer here than it has been before, because the volume of material made it tempting to let the tool do the reading for me.

The research also ended up helping the project for this class. The conclusions I drew from the literature supported our decision to focus on social enterprises, with Kenya as a case study. That was a change we had already made coming out of our Week 7 peer feedback, and this gave us a stronger basis for it.

AI tools this week: ChatGPT and Claude — found relevant research papers; ChatGPT also wrote the prompts I used in NotebookLM. NotebookLM — analyzed themes, research gaps, and potential thesis questions across more than 30 papers I uploaded. What I changed: NotebookLM's first synthesis of themes was very scattered and too broad to use, so I pushed back using my own knowledge of the papers until the response was narrowed down.
